# How to Verify a Case (checklist for Kevin)

Built from the 2026-11-21 session: Llama invented a citation (State v. Schmitt, 541 N.W.2d 652), CourtListener could not find it, and two real cases were then read in full (Rogers v. Moore, Ehle v. Prosser).
This is a research habit, not legal advice. For a serious legal issue, consult a licensed attorney.

## The 7 steps

| # | Step | How | Pass looks like |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Find the exact citation** | Case name, reporter volume and page, court, year. | All five parts present. A missing part gets a placeholder like [CITATION NEEDED]. |
| 2 | **Look it up in a real database** | CourtListener: run the citation checker, then search by citation. | The case name and court match what you were told. |
| 3 | **Compare name and citation** | Does the database return the same case name for that citation? | Same name. A different name or no result is a red flag. |
| 4 | **Read the actual opinion** | Open the full text. Do not rely on a summary. | You read the part that deals with your point. |
| 5 | **Confirm it supports the point** | Find the exact sentence. Quote it exactly. | A quote, not a paraphrase from memory. |
| 6 | **Record the details** | Court, level, date, author, published or unpublished, one sentence on how it supports the point. | Every field filled, or marked [Uncertain]. |
| 7 | **Check the law may have changed** | Look up the current statute. Look for later cases. | Current text read, or listed as missing. |

## Red flags (stop and verify harder)
- **No result** for the citation. This is not proof of invention, since databases have gaps, but treat it as unverified. [Likely]
- **Wrong case type.** Example: "State v." is usually a criminal caption, but the topic was a civil property dispute.
- **Confident tone with no source.** Llama gave a full citation with no hedge.
- **Name matches but court or year does not.**

## Know the limits
- **No Shepard's or KeyCite check.** A case found in CourtListener is not proven to be still good law.
- **Secondary statements.** If a case only repeats a rule from an older case, read the older case too. (Rogers v. Moore repeated the elements; Ehle v. Prosser was the source.)
- **Old cases.** Statutes and later decisions may have changed the rule.
- **Case type matters.** Rogers v. Moore is a prescriptive easement case, not a pure adverse possession case, even though it states the same elements.

## Tags to use
- **[Certain]:** read in the source this session.
- **[Likely]:** reasonable inference.
- **[Uncertain]:** from memory or unverified.
- **Never** present a citation from memory as [Certain].

## Never use for legal research
- Llama or any small local model.
- A citation you cannot find in a real source.

## Reminder before you rely on anything
Verify citations yourself. State what you did not check. Consult a licensed attorney for a serious matter.
